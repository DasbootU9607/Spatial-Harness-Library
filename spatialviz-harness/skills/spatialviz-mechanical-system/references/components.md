# Members, contacts and reference frames

Read direction about each axis from its marked end. In a front view use screen right and screen down. A fixed bearing can locate an axle while leaving spin free. A frame lock sets its spin to zero. A key transfers spin between hubs; concentric sleeves with separate bearings have separate angular velocities. Pitch outlines in the local drawings locate contacts. The printed tooth counts set ratios.

| Member or joint | Working geometry and input | Transmission and drawing requirement |
|---|---|---|
| External wheel, pinion, intermittent tooth | Two engaged outer pitch surfaces with located axes | Match tangents. Opposite spins for parallel axes in the same view. An isolated drive tooth requires its current pocket or a specified dwell restraint. |
| Annular gear | Pinion teeth engage the inside rim | Same relative spin sign. Show the inward tooth boundary. Keep axially separate pinions in distinct detail views. |
| Arm, planet, sun, annulus | The carrier locates moving wheel centers | Subtract the carrier angular speed from each spin before applying the tooth ratio. Restore the frame speed to obtain spin. Ground a member or prescribe a second speed to close a differential. |
| Compound gear, shaft, sleeves | A solid key bridges named hubs; sleeve bearing is independent | Equal signed speed only for a rigid join read from the same end. Connecting both sleeves to the same shaft can make a branched train impossible. |
| Bevel pinion, face wheel, opposed face sectors | Local tangent at the current common tooth and shaft-end viewpoints | The same face wheel presents opposite tangents across its diameter. Mark the active edge and each CW tangent in one contact plane. Do not count apparent overlaps as meshes. |
| Worm and crossed helical members | Helix lead, active flank and shaft-end views | Match velocity normal to the tooth flank. An unwrapped pitch-surface detail exposes handedness; a tooth-count parity rule for parallel gears is insufficient. |
| Rack, rolling pinion | Slider guide and contacted side at a common tooth | Project both velocities on the contact normal. On a grounded bottom rack, CW spin pairs with rightward axle travel. A force location alone does not prescribe either motion. |
| Guide, crank, yoke, rocker | Eccentric pin can slide along a slot; block or bearing constrains the output | Only the component normal to the slot is transmitted. Mark both pivots or the rail and the pin. At zero transverse leverage an instantaneous direction can be singular. Over a cycle, a guided yoke traverses both extremes. |
| Guided slider pair | Two different rails, pins in slotted gear arms | Resolve the first slot, the mesh, then the second slot. Mark the current pin side on each arm and label track directions. |
| Ratchet and sweep lug | Working face, ramp or gap, drive stroke and return | A retreating arm may release; a detent holds a resting gear. Present the correct lug after a half-turn. Specify the stroke length and holding pawl when asking net advance. |
| Rotor pack and reaction housing | A freely pivoted assembly, initial rest and zero external axial torque | Internal acceleration produces opposing angular momentum. The sign follows conservation; a torque-bearing frame or offset pivot needs further information. |
| Cam flank, follower, opposing cam | Preload and signed local radius change around the shown pose | A captive shoe of fixed length closes the gap between opposed profiles. Include a local section or adjacent angular samples. A silhouette alone can conceal the active slope. |
| Spiral slots and common pin | One guide, one marked pin and two local pitch gradients | Both slots must admit the same displacement of the pin. Ignore other crossings. A zero gradient requires a different question. |
| Drum, hanging weight | Taut cord with takeoff at the left or right side | Velocity follows the contact tangent. A clutch permitting free rotation needs a specified holding condition under load. |

## Contrasts observed in the bank

Level0/0 and /2 share the planetary image. Switching which member is grounded changes planet spin relative to orbit. Level0/10 and /12 share the shaft assembly and change the selected side. Level1/28 and /30 switch between an indirect drive and direct lug after the half-turn. Level0/20 and /22 look very different but both transfer velocity through one tooth contact. Level1/16 and /18 use differently shaped green frames yet both constrain the follower to reciprocate. A left/right winding-line counterfactual with the same meshed input is an original opportunity.
