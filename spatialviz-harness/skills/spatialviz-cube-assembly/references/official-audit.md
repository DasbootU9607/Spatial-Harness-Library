# Cube Assembly source audit

The collection calls this task **Cube Assembly** in the display material and `CubeAssembly` in the data. The prefix is `VisualPenetration/CubeAssembly/`. The inventory contains all 80 rows matched by this prefix, including each full source ID, original PNG, original key and source row. The file digest of each original PNG matches its library media filename. The original annotations cite dataset revision `c722d00b2f740eacb1b56f4cbcd3af47bbbe40b8` and code revision `c27f9111a2674f2ea03e2ca1f5833326cf1eec1a`. Each level uses one stem throughout. The released stems call the units equal-sized cubes and omit the number of cubes. Option D reads “All three other options are incorrect.” A drawing scales to fit its panel, so compare grid units, including the partly covered edges between cubes. A target may have an incomplete base row, a low terrace, a raised rail or a tower. The word “complete” denotes the illustrated target. No released stem specifies a motion path, a rotation group, a sliding insertion test, a reflection permission or a fixed camera rule for the player.

### Representative reasoning

- **One given piece.** In Level0/0, Part 1 is flat and has four top faces. Its long leg fits the low terrace of the complete stack. Preserve the raised three-cell turn and the lower step projecting from the front. A retains a low unit where B shows a break under the projecting upper corner. C lacks the forward step. When following cube edges, a cube behind the upper shelf can contribute volume without having an entirely exposed front face. [Original](official/Level0/0-2-3-2-9-2.png)
- **Two given pieces.** In Level1/0, both given pieces are flat three-cell elbows. The target has a corner three levels high. Once the two elbows have been placed, the remaining piece must climb one level and retain the cube at the bend. C is that connected three-cell step. A only spans one level. B loses its face-connected joint. [Original](official/Level1/0-2-3-3-9-3.png)
- **Same volume, wrong form.** In Level1/38 each supplied piece is a three-high column. They can occupy the paired tall columns on the back edge. Three low units must turn around the front of those columns. B has this flat L. C has two cubes. A visibly has three in a straight line. A rotation preserves collinearity and cannot form an elbow. The released explanation says both foils lack a cube. Its description of A disagrees with the picture. [Original](official/Level1/38-2-3-3-9-3.png)
- **Reading a changed view.** Level1/25 gives a dense stack, a corner with a raised cube and a branched part. The keyed piece B has a vertical stem and a side projection near its middle. A retains the stem but loses this projection. The three candidates have visibly different face slopes and shading. Level1/37 also gives C at a conspicuously different camera elevation. Treat unit adjacency as the invariant. The images establish panel orientation, the stem itself establishes no motion convention. [Level1/25](official/Level1/25-2-3-3-16-3.png) · [Level1/37](official/Level1/37-2-3-3-15-3.png)

For a coordinate check of Level1/38, let x run along the paired rear columns and y increase toward the front. The visible lower footprint can be written as (0,0), (1,0), (0,1), (1,1), (0,2). Its height field is 3, 3, 1, 1, 1 in that order. This is a local reconstruction from the picture. Two three-high given pieces fill the columns at y=0. If any three-cell rod were horizontal on an upper level, it would need three collinear occupied cells where only two exist. Thus both upper columns require vertical rods even when rotations are allowed. The three residual cells meet at a right angle and B is the only possible completion.

Across 440 drawing panels, 19 Level0 and 12 Level1 questions have at least one projected cube-edge direction that differs by five degrees or more between panels. A three-degree threshold yields 19 and 16. These are image measurements and provide conservative evidence of viewing changes. Level0/8 shows roughly 36°, 90°, 144° for A/B versus 30°, 90°, 150° for C. Level1/25 spans a 26° change on the steepest varying edge family. The published code supplies a nominal isometric camera; the finished images show the variation. [Per-panel measurements and threshold counts](projection-angles.json)

The observations are readings of the distributed pictures. The released annotations do not supply scene coordinates, hidden-voxel lists or exact camera matrices. An occluded volume inferred from adjoining visible units is a reconstruction. The raw annotation remains beside that reconstruction in the inventory.

## Upstream construction and reproduction

1. `create_freecad/create_visual_penetration/create_cube_assembly.py` builds unit `Part.makeBox` shapes on integer coordinates. Ground rows use nondecreasing lengths along y. Higher rows use the preceding layer and the top layer samples occupied supports. It counts solids, discards stacks below eight, and grows face-connected regions using a queue from a random seed. Every accepted split leaves a connected remainder. Two-part runs request floor(0.6 N) cells in the grown region; three-part runs use floor(0.4 N) for each of the two grown regions. Sorting by size makes the last part the answer. Two foils drop different cubes from that part; dropping an articulation cell may disconnect a foil. [Model entry point](https://github.com/wangst0181/SpatialViz-Bench/blob/c27f9111a2674f2ea03e2ca1f5833326cf1eec1a/create_freecad/create_visual_penetration/create_cube_assembly.py)
2. Each part becomes a compound. The renderer hides the other compounds, calls `viewIsometric()` and `fitAll()`, and saves a 1280 × 1024 PNG. Positions retain their orientations in the scene. Since fit is per panel, the apparent cube size can vary. The script contains no candidate camera randomization.
3. `generate_data.py` contains a commented dispatcher with a misspelled example name relative to the implementation. In `visual_penetration_tasks_utils.py`, the callable is actually `prepare_composite_image_CubeAssemply`. It crops each PNG, shuffles A/B/C, labels the intact largest part with the first letter and assigns the remaining letters to deletions. `create_composite_image_CubeAssembly` draws labels above the upper stack/part row and the lower A/B/C row. `utils.save_json` writes the chosen answer, choices and explanations in `data.json`. D is text only. The source uses “None of the above” and includes N in the question. Its foil explanation contains a literal unexpanded `{choice}`. These differ from the released wording. [Dispatch](https://github.com/wangst0181/SpatialViz-Bench/blob/c27f9111a2674f2ea03e2ca1f5833326cf1eec1a/create_freecad/generate_data.py) · [Composition and key assignment](https://github.com/wangst0181/SpatialViz-Bench/blob/c27f9111a2674f2ea03e2ca1f5833326cf1eec1a/create_freecad/visual_penetration_tasks_utils.py)

The local probe ran the source in FreeCAD 1.0.2 for both split sizes. A host adaptation gives `create_cube` access to the document by declaring `doc` global in `main`. Each accepted document was recomputed and saved as FCStd before closing. The composer was called by its real spelling. The host font resolver allows Arial Bold when DejaVu Sans Bold cannot be loaded. Seeds 74 and 75 produced nine- and sixteen-cell targets, followed by composition seed 79. Open both results to see the unit edges, separate parts and printed answer order. The receipts fingerprint the native files and final PNGs. [Level0 reproduction](upstream-probes/Level0.png) · [Level1 reproduction](upstream-probes/Level1.png) · [Receipt](upstream-probes/receipt.json)

These are mechanism reproductions. The archive does not ship the private random draws or original FCStd files for the released images. For example, Level0/9 and /19 have seven-cell identifiers despite the archived eight-cell minimum. Level1/38 and the camera differences above show further limits to identifying the archived generator with the complete released pipeline.

## Authored rules

A new author can explicitly allow the 24 orientation-preserving lattice rotations and integral translation, require whole-target occupancy and give equal-volume alternatives with conflicting interfaces. State the allowed motions in the prompt. Label hidden layers or add enough structure to reconstruct each piece whenever a single perspective would admit different occupancy. Separate the existence of a legal assembly for each option from the number of placements for that option. The original exercise in Level1/38 already requires recognizing an equal-volume straight piece. An all-equal-volume family or a locally modified interface can extend that reasoning with several candidate interfaces that require exact cover to distinguish.
